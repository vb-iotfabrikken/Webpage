Rolling out 1,400 sensors across 36 schools in Varde Municipality sounded
ambitious on paper. In practice, it came down to a simple formula: one
school per afternoon, for nine weeks straight.

## The setup

Every school got the same basic kit: one gateway in the central hall, one
sensor per classroom and one sensor per shared-use room (staffroom,
multi-purpose hall, music room, library). Each kit was pre-packaged and
labelled with the school name and QR-coded room list.

## The afternoon script

1. **13:00** — arrive at the school. Greet the caretaker, find a power outlet
   near the geometric centre of the building, plug in the gateway.
2. **13:30** — start at the ground floor. Scan the sensor QR code, the room
   QR code, peel, stick, done. Aim for 90 seconds per sensor.
3. **15:00** — coffee with the caretaker. This is not optional; the
   caretaker is the person who will look at the dashboard every day after
   we leave.
4. **15:30** — walk the building one more time, check signal strength in
   the mobile app, move any sensors that show weak signal.
5. **16:00** — done. Drive to the next school.

## What we'd do differently

- **Pre-measure gateway signal.** For two schools we had to come back with a
  second gateway to cover a remote annexe. A 15-minute site survey would
  have caught that.
- **More caretaker onboarding.** 30 minutes of dashboard training per
  caretaker would have paid off immediately. We're now doing a
  region-by-region follow-up specifically on this.
- **Pre-print room labels.** We handwrote room names on 40 sensors across
  the project because the printed labels didn't match the school's own
  room numbering. Small detail, big time saver.

## The data so far

Ten months in, Varde schools have seen average CO₂ above 1,200 ppm drop
from 23 % of teaching hours to 6 %. The ventilation wasn't upgraded — just
the feedback loop. Teachers now open windows on their own initiative, and
the caretakers have prioritised repairs based on the worst-performing
rooms. That's the kind of result that makes the whole nine-week slog
feel worth it.
