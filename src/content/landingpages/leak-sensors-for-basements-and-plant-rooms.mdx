Basements and plant rooms are the most exposed spaces in a building — and at the same time the worst monitored. Most are only visited by a caretaker once a month, and as a result a developing leak is rarely spotted before it has done real damage.

## Typical water sources in plant rooms

- **Water heaters** that suddenly spring a leak after 12–15 years of service.
- **Circulation pumps** that drip from axle seals.
- **Pipe joints** in the main installation — especially near floor penetrations.
- **Drain covers and floor drains** that clog up and cause backup.
- **Air-conditioning and heat exchangers** that condense during warm periods.

## Put sensors where you *don't* want water

We typically recommend 4–8 sensors per plant room:

1. One directly under the water heater.
2. One at the base of the circulation pump.
3. One next to the main inlet stopcock.
4. One at each pipe joint by a floor penetration.
5. One by the drain cover in the floor.
6. One extra near the entrance — for back-flow situations.

Every sensor documents itself on a [floor plan](/en/library/water-detection-stop-damage-before-it-spreads/) in the platform, so new caretakers and technicians can get oriented quickly.

## What do customers use it for?

- **Housing associations** — one sensor per stairwell in the basement, alarms go to the caretaker and a committee mobile.
- **Municipal properties** — a standard pack per school and daycare, integrated with the operations centre.
- **Holiday homes and empty premises** — monitoring of rooms without regular supervision.
- **Hospitals and laboratories** — at critical installations, where downtime is expensive.
